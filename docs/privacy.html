Cue — Privacy Policy

Last updated 22 August 2026

The short version. Cue transcribes conversations using your phone's own offline speech recogniser. The audio never leaves your device. Transcripts are stored only on your phone. Text is sent off the device in exactly one situation: when you personally ask for a summary or ask a question about a conversation — and you can switch that off.

Audio

Cue uses the speech recogniser built into your phone, configured to run offline. Audio is processed on the device and is never recorded to a file, never uploaded, and never sent to us or to anyone else. When "Keep audio on this device" is on — which is the default — Cue will refuse to start recording rather than fall back to a recogniser that would send audio to a server.

Transcripts and notes

Transcripts, summaries, the people you add and any notes you write are stored in a database on your phone. They are not backed up to us, not synced, and not shared. Uninstalling the app removes them. You can delete any single conversation, or everything at once, from Settings.

What is sent, and when

If you ask Cue for a summary of a conversation, or ask a question about one, the text of that transcript is sent to Cue's own service, which passes it to a language model provider (OpenRouter, using a DeepSeek model) to produce the answer. This happens only on that explicit action. It is not used to train any model by us, and we do not store the transcript on our side after answering. Turning off "Allow summaries" in Settings stops this completely — with it off, Cue makes no network requests at all.

What we do not do

  • No accounts, no sign-in, no email address collected.
  • No analytics or tracking SDKs.
  • No advertising, and no ad identifiers.
  • No crash-reporting SDK. (If one is ever added, this policy and the Play data-safety declaration will be updated before that build ships.)
  • No selling or sharing of personal information, because none is collected.

Permissions

  • Microphone — required to hear the conversation you are transcribing. It is requested the first time you tap Listen, and used only while you are recording.
  • Speech recognition — required to turn that audio into text on the device.

Recording other people

Laws about recording conversations differ by country and by state, and some places require the consent of everyone present. Cue does not and cannot check this for you. You are responsible for knowing and following the rules where you are.

Children

Cue is not directed at children under 13 and does not knowingly collect anything from them.

Changes

If this policy changes in a way that affects what leaves your device, the app will be updated to say so, and the date at the top of this page will change.
